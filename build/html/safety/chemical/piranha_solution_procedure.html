Piranha Solution Standard Operating Procedure

Piranha solution is a highly reactive and dangerous mixture used only for cleaning stubborn organic residues off glass. Strict adherence to this Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) is mandatory.

Preparation & Safety Prerequisites

Before handling any chemicals, you must prepare your workspace and don the appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE).

Workspace Preparation

  • Clean Glassware: Pre-wash all glassware with soap and water. Remove all non-glass components, including o-rings, PTFE tape, and screw caps, and wash them separately with soap and water.

  • Clear the Fume Hood: Remove any organic chemicals and all trash from the fume hood, paying special attention to paper towels and KimWipes.

  • Set the Sash: Keep the fume hood sash at the 18-inch mark.

  • Quench Station: Before preparing the solution, prepare 500 mL of milliQ water in a 1000 mL glass beaker, placed inside a secondary container, to quickly quench the glassware if an emergency arises.

Required PPE

  • Goggles and a full-face shield

  • Acid apron worn over a standard lab coat

  • Long pants and close-toed shoes

  • Neoprene gloves worn on top of standard nitrile gloves

Preparing the Solution

Warning

CRITICAL WARNING: Piranha solution must never be removed from the fume hood until it has been completely neutralized.

Mixing Procedure

  1. Pour the sulfuric acid into a glass beaker situated inside a glass secondary container.

  2. Using a clean graduated cylinder, measure out the 30% hydrogen peroxide.

  3. SLOWLY add the hydrogen peroxide to the sulfuric acid using a glass pipette.

  4. Stir the mixture continuously with a glass stirring rod while adding the peroxide to prevent reactive hot spots.

  5. Allow the freshly prepared piranha solution to cool to approximately room temperature before use.

Usage & Warning Signs

Once the solution has cooled, carefully add the glassware to the piranha solution, using stainless steel tongs to manipulate the items if necessary.

  • Expected Reaction: Organic impurities will react exothermically, producing CO2 gas. Allow the glassware to sit in the solution for 12–24 hours to ensure cleaning and to let the solution become slightly less active.

Danger

EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS:

  • Foaming/Smoking: If the solution foams or smokes excessively, wait until it fully stabilizes before adding any more hydrogen peroxide or glassware.

  • Yellow Precipitate: If a yellow precipitate forms at any point, STOP IMMEDIATELY. This indicates the potential accidental formation of explosive organic peroxides (such as TATP). Evacuate the lab immediately and call the University Police at 773-702-8181.

Neutralization and Disposal

  1. Carefully pour the used piranha solution over 5 times its volume in ice.

  2. Slowly add 1 M NaOH to the chilled solution. Note: This neutralization process will be highly exothermic.

  3. Stop adding NaOH once the solution reaches a neutral pH.

  4. Slowly pour the neutralized solution into a designated waste container strictly reserved for piranha solution waste.

  5. Keep the waste container inside a secondary container at all times.

  6. Cap the waste container with a vented lid—or leave it without a lid—in case gas is produced during further decomposition.

  7. Do not store piranha waste for more than 3 months.

Required Fume Hood Signage

When working with Piranha solution, you must display the appropriate signage to alert other lab members.

  1. When Piranha Solution is IN USE: Print page 1 of this document and display it clearly on the fume hood.

  2. When Piranha Solution is NOT IN USE: Print page 2 of this document and display it clearly on the fume hood.
