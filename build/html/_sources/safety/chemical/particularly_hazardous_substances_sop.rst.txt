============================================
Particularly Hazardous Substances (PHS) SOP
============================================

Particularly Hazardous Substances (PHS) require careful planning, proper protective equipment, and strict adherence to safety protocols. This page outlines the specific PHS present in our laboratory, their associated risks, and the required guidelines for handling them safely.

----------------------------------
PHS Inventory and Associated Risks
----------------------------------

The following table details the PHS currently utilized in our lab and the specific health hazards they present:

.. list-table::
   :widths: 40 60
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Chemical Name
     - Primary Hazards & Associated Risks
   * - 1-methyl-2-pyrrolidinone (NMP)
     - Reproductive hazard.
   * - 2-ethoxyethanol
     - Highly toxic; reproductive hazard and causes organ damage; peroxide former.
   * - 2-methoxyethanol
     - Highly toxic; reproductive hazard and causes organ damage; peroxide former.
   * - Chloroform
     - Toxic; causes dizziness, nausea, unconsciousness, and death; possible carcinogen.
   * - Dichloromethane (DCM)
     - Toxic; extreme skin and eye irritant; possible carcinogen. Highly volatile (Tb = 39°C).
   * - Diglyme
     - Reproductive toxin; peroxide former.
   * - Dimethylformamide (DMF)
     - Liver and reproductive toxin.
   * - Ethylene glycol
     - Reproductive toxin; peroxide former.
   * - Formaldehyde solution
     - Known carcinogen (linked to nasopharyngeal cancer and leukemia).
   * - Toluene
     - Neurotoxin and reproductive toxin.

--------------------------
General Handling Guidelines
--------------------------

When working with any of the substances listed above, you must adhere to the following preparatory and procedural steps:

* **Review:** Read the Safety Data Sheet (SDS/MSDS) closely prior to handling the chemical.
* **Substitute:** Substitute the chemical with a less hazardous alternative whenever possible.
* **Plan:** Plan your experiment out thoroughly before beginning any physical work.
* **Buddy System:** Complete your procedure only when other lab mates are present in the room.
* **Timing:** Ensure the first few iterations of any new procedure are conducted during standard work hours.
* **Scale:** Minimize the quantity of chemicals used; never prepare or use more than is strictly needed for your procedure.

--------------------------
Engineering Controls & PPE
--------------------------

Proper engineering controls and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) are mandatory to prevent exposure:

* **Ventilation:** Always utilize the fume hood or glovebox, as most of these substances are volatile and pose severe inhalation hazards.
* **Hand Protection:** Always wear gloves when handling any PHS.
* **Layering:** Consider double-gloving to provide an extra physical barrier against chemical permeation.
* **Compatibility:** Consult the Safety Glove Selection Guide to ensure your gloves are made of a compatible material for the specific chemical you are handling.

------------------------------
Emergency Response to Exposure
------------------------------

In the event of accidental contact with a PHS, take immediate action:

* **Glove Contamination:** If a PHS contacts your glove, immediately remove the gloves and thoroughly wash your hands.
* **Skin Contact:** If a PHS penetrates your glove and makes contact with your skin, continuously wash the affected area for 15 minutes.
* **Medical Review:** Seek medical attention if you experience any burning, irritation, or abnormal symptoms.
* **Needle Stick Hotline:** For further medical guidance, contact the Needle Stick Hotline at **773-753-1880**.
* **Poison Control:** You can also contact the Poison Help Line at **1-800-222-1222** for emergency exposure assistance.
